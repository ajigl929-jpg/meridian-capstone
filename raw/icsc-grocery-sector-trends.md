## Summary as fetched

### Market overview
The U.S. grocery sector represents a $912.4 billion market experiencing
significant reinvention. Retailers are reshaping formats, footprints, and
brand identities to remain competitive as shopping habits evolve.

### Specialty grocers gaining share
Specialty grocers outperform traditional supermarkets. Trader Joe's
achieved 3% year-over-year growth (ending Feb. 28), surpassing the
overall sector by 6 percentage points, with "double-digit year-over-year
growth among Gen Z shoppers." Traditional operators like Publix and
Safeway experience pullbacks, particularly among lower-income shoppers.

### Major retailers repositioning
- **Target**: Repositioning its $24 billion grocery business as a
  "distinctive grocery destination" emphasizing emerging brands and
  wellness products, described as "delightful, joyful, unlike what
  you'll find anywhere else."
- **Walmart**: Remodeling 650+ stores and opening ~20 new locations in
  2026-2027; allocated $5.5 billion for 675 remodels in fiscal 2026.
- **Costco**: Targeting 30+ new stores annually; expects 28 net openings
  in fiscal 2026; operates nearly 640 U.S. stores.

### Format innovation
Aldi tested flexible modular layouts in Aventura, Florida (beginning
September 2025) enabling adaptation to urban and suburban footprints.
The chain opened 150 U.S. stores in 2026, marking its 50th anniversary.

Gelson's launched 5,400-square-foot micromarkets pairing premium
convenience shopping with EV charging infrastructure.

### Investment activity
Grocery-anchored center deals totaled nearly $11 billion in 2025 — a
42% increase from 2024. Notable transactions:
- Brixton Capital: $130 million across two centers
- Nuveen: $298 million for a seven-property portfolio
- Blackstone Real Estate: $6.7 billion across three portfolios (130
  properties)

### Competition pressures
A Tillster survey indicates 29% of diners visit fast-food chains less
frequently, while 36% visit grocery stores more often and 33% visit
convenience stores more frequently.
