# Grocery Industry Trends

Type: concept | Last updated: 2026-09-11
Sources: [raw/icsc-grocery-sector-trends.md](../../../raw/icsc-grocery-sector-trends.md) (ICSC Exchange, retrieved 2026-09-11)

External market research, not Meridian-specific — useful context for
[Expansion Strategy](expansion-strategy.md) and for framing interview
questions in [Interview Questions](../analysis/interview-questions.md).

## Market overview

The U.S. grocery sector is a $912.4 billion market; retailers broadly
are reshaping formats, footprints, and brand identity to compete as
shopping habits shift.

## Specialty grocers gaining share

Specialty grocers are outperforming traditional supermarkets. Trader
Joe's grew 3% year-over-year (through Feb. 28), 6 points above the
overall sector, with double-digit growth among Gen Z shoppers.
Traditional operators like Publix and Safeway are seeing pullbacks,
particularly among lower-income shoppers. This is directly relevant to
[Meridian Markets](../entities/meridian-markets.md)'s positioning as a
specialty grocer competing against larger, more traditional chains.

## Major retailers repositioning

Target, Walmart, and Costco are all investing heavily in
repositioning or expanding their grocery footprints — Target toward a
"distinctive grocery destination" identity, Walmart via 650+ store
remodels ($5.5B in fiscal 2026) plus ~20 new stores through 2027,
Costco via 30+ new stores/year.

## Format innovation

Smaller, more flexible formats are emerging: Aldi's modular layout
test (Aventura, FL, since Sept. 2025) adapts to urban and suburban
footprints; Gelson's launched 5,400-sq-ft micromarkets with EV
charging. Relevant to how a next Meridian location (e.g. Pasadena)
might be sized or positioned.

## Investment activity

Grocery-anchored retail center deals totaled nearly $11 billion in
2025, up 42% from 2024 — signaling strong investor appetite for
grocery-anchored real estate, the same category Meridian operates in
via leased sites.

## Competition pressures

A Tillster survey found diners shifting away from fast food toward
grocery and convenience stores (36% and 33% report visiting each more
often, respectively) — a tailwind for grocery generally, including
prepared foods, one of Meridian's stated differentiators.
