## Industry-wide, from the ICSC article (fully ingested)

- Aldi tested a flexible modular store layout in Aventura, Florida,
  starting September 2025, designed to adapt to both urban and
  suburban footprints, and opened 150 U.S. stores in 2026 overall (its
  50th-anniversary year) — no other specific city/region named for
  those openings. — `raw/icsc-grocery-sector-trends.md`, via
  [Grocery Industry Trends](../concepts/grocery-industry-trends.md)
- Gelson's launched 5,400-sq-ft micromarkets with EV charging — no
  location given in what we captured. — `raw/icsc-grocery-sector-trends.md`
- Trader Joe's grew 3% YoY (through Feb 28), 6 points above the
  sector, with double-digit growth among Gen Z shoppers — this is
  growth/traffic data, not opening-location data. —
  `raw/icsc-grocery-sector-trends.md`

## Conclusion (Claude's own — not stated in any source)

The only two sources with real specificity both point toward
specialty grocers concentrating openings/competition in already-dense
coastal California and Sun Belt-adjacent metro markets (LA for
Bristol Farms/Erewhon, Meridian's own LA/Orange/Ventura footprint,
Aldi's Florida test) rather than a broad national pattern — but this
is a thin read from two data points, not something any source
asserts directly. It would need the actual JLL, Grocery Dive, and
SafeGraph reports ingested (all still on the fallback list, per
[Source Tracking](source-tracking.md)) before treating "where" as
well-supported.
